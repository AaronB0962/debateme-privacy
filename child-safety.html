Child safety standards

DebateMe is made by BeachApplications. This page describes our standards against child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM), and how to report a concern.

Last updated: September 30, 2026

Our standard

We have zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation. Content or behavior that sexualizes, exploits, endangers, or abuses children is not allowed anywhere in DebateMe, including debates, arguments, chat messages, profile names, bios, and profile pictures.

DebateMe is for adults only. You must be 18 or older to use it. The app's Engagement Rules, shown to every new user and always available from the Debates tab, prohibit sexual content and anything illegal.

How to report a concern

  • In the app: tap Report on any debate, argument, chat message, or profile. Choose Illegal content as the reason, add details if you can, and send it. The person you report is not told who reported them.
  • By email: write to beachmadeapps@gmail.com with the subject Child safety concern. Include your debate name, the name of the account involved, and a description of what you saw.

If a child is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first.

What we do when we receive a report

  1. A DebateMe administrator reviews the report promptly.
  2. Content that violates these standards is removed right away.
  3. The account responsible is permanently banned.
  4. We report apparent child sexual abuse material to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) through its CyberTipline, and to law enforcement, as the law requires.
  5. We keep the relevant records only as long as the law requires or as law enforcement requests.

Tools for users

  • Report any post, message, or profile from within the app.
  • Block any person from the Account tab. Blocked people's debates are hidden from you.
  • Delete your account at any time from the Account tab. See how account deletion works.

Compliance and contact

We comply with applicable child safety laws, including the requirement to report apparent child sexual abuse material to the authorities.
